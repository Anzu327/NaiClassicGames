ABOUT THE WORK

NAI WA AND THE SHAPE OF ADAPTATION

WHAT IS NAI WA?

Nai Wa (奶蛙) is a mysterious yellow object. On the Chinese internet, it has slowly moved away from its original image and become an online symbol that people can reuse in many different ways.

People place it into all kinds of unrelated situations—sometimes because it looks funny, and sometimes because they simply do not know what else to put there. Over time, Nai Wa has become a very random but surprisingly useful “answer”.

Even when its body is squeezed, stretched, pixelated, or reduced to a yellow block and two green eyes, it remains recognizable. Its body is no longer defined by one fixed shape; it has become a visual material that can keep changing its form.

BEING FORCED TO ADAPT

This work places Nai Wa inside the rules of four classic video games: Snake, Pac-Man, Flappy Bird, and Tetris. Their structures are clear and rigid, so Nai Wa must change its body and movements to fit each one.

There is something strange and funny about this situation. Nai Wa always looks a little confused, as if it does not understand what is happening, yet its body has already begun adapting to the environment.

In real life, we also keep entering new rules and systems. Sometimes, before we fully understand what is happening, we already have to take our place and do what is expected of us. Nai Wa makes this situation more direct—and more absurd.

It can appear on any screen, in any game, and in any group of pixels. It keeps changing, but it is still Nai Wa.

When adapting becomes an instinct, do we still remember what shape we originally had?

In the end, perhaps everyone has somehow become a yellow piece of 💩.
